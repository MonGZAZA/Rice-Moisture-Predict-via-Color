Stage 1 · Section IV.A & IV.B

Data Acquisition & Border Cropping

Each high-resolution image (4896 × 3264) is captured inside a controlled 20 × 20 cm lightbox and cropped to bounding box (586, 0, 4252, 3164) (3666 × 3164 px) to remove lightbox walls and retain only the rice paddy region.

Stage 2 · Section IV.B (Fig. 3)

HSV Hue-Only Band-Stop Artifact Removal

To suppress non-paddy elements or noise (such as blue/cyan color patches or background specks), the cropped RGB image is converted to HSV and pixels falling inside the fixed Hue reject band H ∈ [100, 140] (OpenCV 0–179 scale) are masked to black (0, 0, 0).

Stage 3 · Section IV.C (Fig. 4)

HSV Color-Splitting: Green vs. Brown Husk Segments

Higher moisture preserves chlorophyll pigments and surface gloss, whereas dry husks shift toward dull brown. We decouple hue from illumination in HSV space and apply in-range thresholding with H ∈ [25, 85], S ∈ [35, 255], V ∈ [35, 255] to isolate the Green segment; the Brown segment is defined as its complement among valid paddy pixels.

36 of the 38 features with |r| ≥ 0.80 come from the Green segment (and 2 from the Full image), confirming that green-region optical properties carry the primary moisture signal.

Stage 4 · Section IV.D, IV.E & V.A

Color Feature Extraction & Correlation Screening (243 → 38 Predictors)

81 color features are extracted from each of the 3 images (Full, Green segment, Brown segment = 243 total variables). Screening for |r| ≥ 0.80 and p < 0.01 retains the 38 predictors below. Each predictor xj is standardized using the training set (N = 251) mean μj and standard deviation σj: zj = (xj − μj) / σj.
